VILLAGE GEOGRAPHY

Village Geography The mountain to the north of Alchi is known as Sportsey. Just above the bridge lies an old pass called Kinbi-la, and beyond it stands an independent peak named Khar-i-dak, atop which the rTe-lamo Lhato is located. To the west is the mountain Sur-i-thhed, while the southwest is marked by Shugpa Szguru. In the south rises Samar-i-kem kem, also referred to as rDzong, and on its summit rests the Lhato of Daltong sRhungma. Toward the southeast, an old route leads to Sumda village via the mountain pass Skas-pe La. Most of the mountains associated with Alchi are situated in this southeastern direction, including Tashi Sangrub, Yak-Khang, Gyapo Dinjang, Tshermangchan, Spartsun, and Bigutsey. To the east lie the peaks of Tshiril and Dag-i-Banga

STORY ABOUT THE SKANGRINGS - DOK

There is a well-known local lore about the protector deity of the valley known as Skangrings Dok. The deity is believed to reside within a juniper tree and is revered by the name Ama Naga rGyalmo—the Naga Queen Mother. According to the tale, she once visited the valley disguised as a long-legged nun, making it impossible for the villagers to recognize her. As a result, no one offered her the customary holy offerings. Angered by this neglect, she opened the lake situated at the far end of the valley, unleashing a flood that submerged the entire region of rDzabar. The deluge was so immense that even the deity Bang Lhamo had to flee by climbing an old tree to escape the rising waters. Because Ama Naga rGyalmo appeared in the form of a long-legged nun, the valley came to be named Skangrings—from skang meaning leg, and rings meaning long—commemorating her distinctive appearance.
